# Interview Proof Points

## Tell Me About Yourself

I am a healthcare administration student building a portfolio around healthcare workflow intelligence, revenue cycle operations, patient access, prior authorization, eligibility verification, documentation quality, and denial prevention. My focus is understanding where workflows break upstream before those issues become billing problems, rework, delayed reimbursement, or patient confusion.

## Why Healthcare Administration

Healthcare administration gives me a way to connect patient experience with operational improvement. I am interested in how better workflows, clearer ownership, stronger documentation, and better visibility can reduce friction for both staff and patients.

## Strongest Workflow Insight

Most revenue cycle problems do not begin in billing. Many begin earlier in the workflow during intake, eligibility verification, prior authorization, documentation, or handoffs.

## How I Would Reduce Denials

I would start by categorizing denials by reason, payer, service line, and upstream workflow source. Then I would identify repeat patterns and recommend control points such as eligibility rechecks, authorization trackers, documentation reviews, or clearer handoff ownership.

## How I Approach Process Improvement

I look for where information loses accuracy, timing, ownership, visibility, or trust. Then I connect the breakdown to measurable impact such as rework, denials, days in A/R, escalation volume, or patient confusion.

## How Patient Experience Connects to Revenue Cycle

Patients often feel the impact of operational breakdowns through delays, repeated calls, unclear billing, surprise balances, or confusion about coverage. A stronger revenue cycle workflow can protect both financial performance and patient trust.
