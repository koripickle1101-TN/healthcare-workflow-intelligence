# Prior Authorization Workflow Breakdown

## Problem

Prior authorization problems often look like payer problems at the surface, but many of them are workflow visibility problems. When authorization requirements, status, ownership, due dates, and clinical documentation needs are not visible in one place, staff are forced to rely on memory, messages, manual checking, or last-minute escalation.

## Where the Workflow Breaks

The workflow breaks when scheduling, clinical documentation, payer requirements, and authorization follow-up are disconnected. A service may move forward before the authorization status is clear, or staff may not know who owns the next step.

## Root Cause

The root cause is usually missing ownership and status visibility. The workflow does not clearly show what is required, who is responsible, what is pending, when it is due, and what happens if the authorization is not complete on time.

## Operational Impact

Poor authorization visibility can create delayed services, rescheduled appointments, denial risk, staff escalation, duplicate phone calls, manual tracking spreadsheets, and patient frustration. The team spends time chasing status instead of working from a reliable system.

## Patient Impact

Patients may experience delays, uncertainty, cancelled visits, repeated questions, or unexpected financial risk if authorization requirements are not handled clearly before service delivery.

## Recommended Fix

Create a prior authorization tracker with the following fields: patient account, payer, service, authorization required, documentation needed, owner, status, submission date, due date, payer response, escalation needed, and final clearance status.

## Metrics to Watch

- Authorization turnaround time
- Number of pending authorizations
- Authorization-related denials
- Rescheduled services due to authorization delay
- Escalation frequency
- Manual follow-up volume

## Portfolio Insight

Prior authorization is not only about payer approval. It is a workflow control system that depends on timing, ownership, documentation, and visibility.
